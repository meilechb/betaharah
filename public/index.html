A mikvah calendar you update by text message

Text one digit when something happens. Betaharah works out the onot perishah, the hefsek taharah, the seven clean days and mikvah night by your customs, shows them in your own calendar, and reminds you by text.

How it works

  1. Sign up with your phone number

    A code is texted to you and you type it in. Nothing else is asked: no name, no email address.

  2. Choose your location and customs

    Sunrise, sunset and nightfall are worked out for your location. Go over the customs with your rav.

  3. Record dates as they happen

    Text one digit, or enter it on the website. Every text gets a reply that says what was recorded.

  4. See the dates, get reminders

    If you want: the dates in your calendar, reminders by text, and your spouse's phone on the same account.

Two ways to use it

One digit for each thing that happens

What each text means
TextMeaning
1A flow began
2Hefsek taharah done today
3The count of clean days was broken
4Immersed tonight
5A stain that makes her niddah was found
9Where things stand
0Undo the last entry

You can say when it happened: 1 night, 1 yesterday night or 1 6:40am. If a text arrives shortly after sunrise or sunset, the reply says which side it was counted on. The Help page has every detail.

In your own calendar

  • Google Calendar. Connect your Google account. The site creates one calendar there and keeps it up to date. It can see and change only that one calendar.
  • Apple Calendar, Outlook, or any calendar app. Add a private subscription link. Anyone who has the link can read the calendar; you can replace or remove it at any time.
  • Words or symbols. Event titles can be written out, or shown as discreet symbols for a calendar other people may see. Halachic terms can be in English letters or in Hebrew.

What it calculates

The onot perishah

Onah beinonit, veset hachodesh and veset haflagah, by day or by night as the Hebrew date and the sun require, with each reason written out.

Hefsek taharah and the seven clean days

The first day the hefsek can be done and its time before sunset, then each of the seven days, and what changes if the count is broken.

Mikvah night

The night and the time of nightfall for your location, moved past Yom Kippur and Tisha B'Av, with a note when it is Shabbat or Yom Tov.

Every point on which customs differ is a setting

  • Five days or four before the hefsek
  • Onah beinonit on day 30, day 31, or both
  • Onah beinonit and veset hachodesh for one onah or the whole day
  • The onah of the Or Zarua, observed or not
  • The veset haflagah counted in days, or in onot from the hefsek
  • Nightfall: degrees below the horizon, or minutes after sunset
  • Yom Tov in Israel or outside it
  • Long cycles, a flow on the 30th, and longer intervals not yet passed

Until you save your choices, the calendar uses the stricter option of each. Review them with your rav.

Private

  • A phone number is all it asks for. No name, no email address, no date of birth.
  • Each account's data is encrypted (AES-256-GCM) before it is stored.
  • No advertising, no analytics and no tracking. Nothing on these pages is loaded from another site.
  • Your information is not sold, rented or given to anyone.
  • You can download everything kept about your account, or delete the account, at any time under Settings.

What encryption does not do

The server holds the key, because it has to read your dates to work out the calendar and send reminders. So the person who runs the site has the technical means to read an account. Text messages pass through Telnyx and the phone companies, who handle the number and the words of each text.

What it does not do

It does not rule on halachic questions. It keeps track of dates by the customs you choose. For stains, for anything unclear, and when a veset kavua may have been established (the calendar points it out and does not apply it), ask your rav.

It is not medical advice. Text messages and calendar updates can be late or fail to arrive, so check what it shows.

Text messages

Betaharah sends a sign-in code when you ask for one, replies to the messages you text it (including JOIN, if you sign up by text), and sends reminders only if you turn them on in your settings. Message frequency varies. Message and data rates may apply. Reply STOP to stop, HELP for help. See the Terms and the Privacy page.
