From one entry

Record when a period begins. Everything after it is worked out.

Each date is set by the Hebrew calendar, which begins at sunset, and by sunrise and sunset calculated for your own location.

  1. Flow

    You record it: one tap, or text 1. Day or night is decided by sunset where you are.

  2. H

    Hefsek taharah

    The first day it can be done, after five days or four as you choose, and the time of sunset that day.

  3. 7

    Seven clean days

    Each day with its two bedikot, days 1 and 7 marked as required. A flow in between voids the count.

  4. M

    Mikvah night

    The time of nightfall, moved past Yom Kippur and Tisha B'Av, with a note on Shabbat and Yom Tov nights.

  5. Onot perishah

    Onah beinonit, veset hachodesh and veset haflagah, each in its onah, by day or by night, with the reason written out.

Recording

One digit, sent as a text. A reply that says exactly what was recorded.

Text 1 when a period begins, 2 after the hefsek, 4 after immersion. Every reply states the date it recorded and what follows from it. 9 tells you where things stand, and 0 undoes the last entry.

  • 1Flow began
  • 2Hefsek done
  • 3Count broken
  • 4Immersed
  • 5Stain
  • 9Status
  • 0Undo

Say when it happened: 1 night, 1 yesterday night, 1 6:40am, or a past date such as 1 11/22 night. A text that is not understood changes nothing, and the reply says what to send. On the website, the same entries are one tap each.

Your customs

Calculated by your customs.

Wherever customs differ, there is a setting: twelve in all, from the days of waiting before the hefsek to the time of nightfall.

  • Five days or four before the hefsek
  • Onah beinonit on day 30, day 31, or both, for one onah or the whole day
  • Or Zarua, observed or not
  • Veset haflagah in days, or in onot from the hefsek (Chabad custom)
  • A flow on the 30th, long cycles, and longer intervals not yet passed
  • Yom Tov in Israel or outside it; nightfall by degrees or minutes

Your calendar

In the calendar you already use.

Connect Google Calendar, or add a private link to Apple Calendar, Outlook or any calendar app. The dates arrive as events, written out, or as symbols only, for a calendar other people may see.

  • Google Calendar

    The site creates one new calendar in your Google account and keeps it up to date. It can see and change only that calendar.

  • Apple Calendar, Outlook, any app

    A private subscription link. Anyone with the link can read the calendar, so it can be replaced or removed at any time.

  • English letters or Hebrew

    Halachic terms as hefsek taharah or as הפסק טהרה, on the site and in the calendar.

Reminders

A text before each thing that has a time.

Before sunset on the day of the hefsek. Each morning and afternoon of the clean days, for the bedikot. Mikvah night, and the next morning if no immersion was recorded. Before each onah. Each phone turns on the kinds it wants, and when.

Nothing is sent on Shabbat or Yom Tov. Here, clean day 7 is on Shabbat and mikvah night is Saturday evening, so both reminders came on Friday afternoon, before Shabbat.

Reminders are off until you turn them on. Discreet wording sends letters and times only: H Sat Dec 5 before 4:31 PM. Reply 2 when done. Reply STOP to stop every text.

Husband and wife

One account, each on your own phone.

Both of you see the same calendar, record from your own phone, and choose your own reminders. To add the second phone, one of you texts ADD and gets a code; the other phone texts JOIN with that code. On the website, a code is texted to the new phone.

Up to four phones share an account. Any of them can be removed, and a phone can leave by texting LEAVE.

No internet needed

Everything works by text message, from any phone that can text.

No website, no app, no browser. Text JOIN, answer three questions, and the account is set up. After that, recording, status, customs, reminders, a second phone, the calendar link and deleting the account are all done by text.

  1. 1Sign up

    1. You send: JOIN
    2. Reply: Welcome to Betaharah. This phone is signed up. Step 1 of 3: reply with your ZIP code (in Canada, your postal code, e.g. H3W 1A1). It sets the times of sunset.

  2. 2Location and customs

    1. You send: 08701
    2. Reply: Location set: Lakewood, NJ 08701. Sunset and nightfall are now worked out for it.
    3. Reply: Step 2 of 3: customs. … A: keep them for now. B: answer the 12 questions by text. C: do it later on the website. Reply A, B or C.

  3. 3The last period

    1. You send: 1 11/1 night
    2. Reply: Recorded: flow Sun Nov 1 night, 2026. Hefsek from Fri Nov 6. 0 to undo. …
    3. You send: DONE
    4. Reply: Setup is finished. From now on text 1 when a flow begins, 2 after the hefsek, 4 after immersion, 9 for status. …

Privacy

Private by design.

  • Only what signing in needs

    A phone number is enough. Signing in with Google keeps the email address and name on that Google account, used only to recognize you.

  • Private to your account

    Your calendar is shown only to the phones on your account, and to a calendar link if you make one.

  • No ads, no tracking

    Your information is never sold or given to anyone.

  • Download or delete, any time

    Take a copy of everything from Settings. Delete the account there, or by texting DELETE ACCOUNT.

  • Discreet when you want it

    Calendar events as symbols, and reminders in letters and times only.

  • Quiet on Shabbat and Yom Tov

    No reminder is sent on Shabbat or Yom Tov.

Questions

Questions people ask.

More on the Help page.

Is it free?

Yes. There is no charge and no advertising. Your mobile carrier's message and data rates may apply to texts.

Which customs does it support?

Twelve settings, each with the options found in practice:

  • Five days or four before the hefsek
  • Onah beinonit on day 30, day 31, or both; for one onah or the whole day
  • Veset hachodesh for one onah or the whole day
  • The onah of the Or Zarua, observed or not
  • Veset haflagah counted in days, or in onot from the hefsek (Shulchan Aruch HaRav; Chabad custom)
  • Longer intervals not yet passed, observed or not
  • A flow on the 30th when the next month has 29 days
  • Cycles that regularly run past day 31
  • Pausing vesatot
  • Yom Tov in Israel or outside it
  • Nightfall: 8.5 or 7.083 degrees, 50 or 72 minutes, or another time set on the website

It does not apply or uproot a veset kavua, calculate vesatot under hormonal contraception, or handle the rules of a kallah, childbirth or a medical procedure.

Does it send anything on Shabbat or Yom Tov?

No reminder is sent on Shabbat or Yom Tov. One that would fall then is sent beforehand, held until afterwards, or left out, depending on what it is.

Do I need a smartphone, or the internet?

No. Any phone that can send and receive text messages can sign up and do everything by text. The website, for those who use one, works on any phone or computer with a browser.

Who can see my data?

Your calendar is shown only to the phones on your account, and to anyone you give your calendar link to. It is never sold or given to anyone. The Privacy page says exactly what is kept.

Can I use it with Google Calendar, or on an iPhone?

Yes. Connect Google Calendar from Settings, and the dates appear in a calendar of their own there. For Apple Calendar on an iPhone, iPad or Mac, or for Outlook, add your private calendar link.

Does it work outside the United States?

On the website, any place can be chosen, and its times are calculated. By text, a location is set with a US ZIP code or a Canadian postal code; for another country, text WEB for a link to set it on the website. At the moment, signing up needs a US or Canadian phone number (one that begins with +1).

Is it in Hebrew?

The site is in English. A setting writes the halachic terms in English letters or in Hebrew, on the site and in the calendar. Text messages are always in English letters.
